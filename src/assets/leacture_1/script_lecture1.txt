Script: The Cognito Times - A Semantic News Recommendation SystemBased on the report by Group 1 (Gujarati, More, & Patel)[SCENE 1: THE PROBLEM]Narrator:
Every single day, approximately 3 million news articles are published online. For the average reader, this isn't just a lot of content—it’s "information overload".+2Narrator:
Traditional recommendation systems try to help, but they have major flaws. Collaborative Filtering fails when a new user joins (the "cold start" problem). Meanwhile, traditional Content-Based Filtering relies on simple keyword matching (like TF-IDF), which misses the actual meaning behind the words.+1Narrator:
Today, we are presenting "The Cognito Times"—a next-generation recommendation system that uses Sentence-BERT (SBERT) to understand not just what you click, but what you actually care about.+1[SCENE 2: WHY SBERT?]Narrator:So, why did we choose Sentence-BERT?Narrator:
You might ask, "Why not use standard BERT? It's the state-of-the-art." The answer is speed. Standard BERT uses a cross-encoder architecture. If you wanted to find the most similar pair of sentences in a collection of 10,000, standard BERT would require 50 million computations and take roughly 65 hours.+1Narrator:That is impossible for real-time news.Narrator:
Our solution uses SBERT (specifically the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model). It utilizes Siamese networks to process text into fixed vectors independently. This reduces the search time from hours to mere seconds, maintaining accuracy while drastically lowering computational cost.+2[SCENE 3: THE RICH USER PROFILE]Narrator:
The core innovation of this system is how we model the user. We don't just look at one metric. We construct a "Rich User Profile" using a technique called Weighted Vector Fusion.+1Narrator:
Instead of a simple list of tags, we generate a user vector ($u$) by combining three distinct data points:Explicit Preferences ($V_{cat}$): The categories you say you like (e.g., Technology, Business).Explicit Feedback ($V_{like}$): The articles you have actively "Liked."Implicit History ($V_{hist}$): What you have passively read, with a time-decay factor applied so old news counts for less.Narrator:We fuse these together using a specific weighted formula:$$u = 0.2(V_{cat}) + 0.6(V_{like}) + 0.2(V_{hist})$$Narrator:
Notice the 0.6 weight on Likes. This ensures the system prioritizes what you actively engage with over what you passively scroll past.[SCENE 4: THE PIPELINE & DIVERSITY]Narrator:Here is how the system works in real-time:Ingestion: We fetch live articles from the US, UK, and Canada via the Event Registry API, filtering out paywalls.+1Vectorization: We convert the article title and body into a 384-dimensional dense vector using SBERT.Ranking with MMR: This is crucial. If we just sorted by similarity, you might see five articles about the exact same breaking news event.Narrator:
To fix this, we use Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR). This algorithm selects articles that are similar to you, but dissimilar to articles already in your feed. This ensures your feed is relevant, but also diverse.+1[SCENE 5: EVALUATION & RESULTS]Narrator:
We tested "The Cognito Times" against two other methods: a Classical Hybrid model (TF-IDF/Word2Vec) and a Standard BERT model.Narrator:The results were clear:Classical Model: Very fast (0.12s) but "dumb"—it struggled with context.Standard BERT: Smart, but too slow (1.21s) for real-time scaling.SBERT (Our System): The winner. It achieved optimal accuracy with a sub-second response time of 0.94 seconds.Narrator:
Visualizing the vectors showed us why: SBERT produced a perfect Gaussian distribution of data, meaning it was efficiently packing semantic meaning into the vector space, unlike the sparse signals of the classical model.+1[SCENE 6: CONCLUSION]Narrator:
In conclusion, "The Cognito Times" successfully bridges the gap between high-accuracy deep learning and low-latency user experience. By leveraging Weighted Vector Fusion and MMR, we have built a system that doesn't just match keywords—it understands what you actually want to read.+1Narrator:
